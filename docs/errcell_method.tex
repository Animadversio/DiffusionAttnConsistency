\section{Error Cell Confidence Analysis}

\subsection{Setup}

We investigate whether cells that cause rule violations in the model's decoded output
are systematically less confident than correct cells.
The analysis is applied to Latin square samples from two encoding families:
one-hot and scalar.

\subsection{Confidence Estimation}

\paragraph{One-hot encoding.}
The model produces an $N \times n \times n^2$ tensor in which the $n$ channels over
each of the $n^2$ cell positions form a soft one-hot vector.
Per-cell confidence is defined as
\begin{equation}
  c_{ij} = \frac{\max_k \, x_{ijk} - x_{\text{inactive}}}{x_{\text{active}} - x_{\text{inactive}}} \in [0,1],
  \label{eq:onehot_conf}
\end{equation}
where $x_{\text{active}}$ and $x_{\text{inactive}}$ are the encoding's target values
for the active and inactive channels, respectively (see Table~\ref{tab:encodings}).
A value $c_{ij}=1$ indicates a perfectly sharp one-hot; $c_{ij}=0$ indicates a
completely flat distribution.
The decoded integer is $\hat{y}_{ij} = \arg\max_k x_{ijk}$ (no threshold applied
for this analysis).

\paragraph{Scalar encoding.}
The model outputs a scalar per cell.
Valid quantization levels are $v_k = k/(n-1)$ for $k=0,\ldots,n-1$.
Per-cell confidence is
\begin{equation}
  c_{ij} = 1 - \frac{\min_k |x_{ij} - v_k|}{\Delta/2},
  \label{eq:scalar_conf}
\end{equation}
where $\Delta = 1/(n-1)$ is the level spacing.
Here $c_{ij}=1$ means the cell lies exactly on a valid level; $c_{ij}=0$ at the
midpoint between two levels; $c_{ij}<0$ means the cell is unsnappable.
Samples where any cell is unsnappable (distance $> \varepsilon$, default $\varepsilon=0.15$)
are excluded from analysis.

\subsection{Error Cell Identification}

A cell at grid position $(r,c)$ in sample $i$ is an \emph{error cell} if its decoded
symbol appears more than once in row $r$ \emph{or} more than once in column $c$.
Both copies of a duplicated symbol are flagged.

Formally, define $\hat{y}_{i,r,c}$ as the decoded integer at position $(r,c)$.
Then
\begin{equation}
  \text{err}_{i,r,c} = \mathbf{1}\!\left[
    \sum_{c'} \mathbf{1}[\hat{y}_{i,r,c'} = \hat{y}_{i,r,c}] > 1
    \;\vee\;
    \sum_{r'} \mathbf{1}[\hat{y}_{i,r',c} = \hat{y}_{i,r,c}] > 1
  \right].
\end{equation}

\subsection{Uncertainty Comparison}

We define \emph{uncertainty} as $u_{ij} = 1 - c_{ij}$ and split all cells from the
last $K$ checkpoints into correct cells ($\text{err}=0$) and error cells
($\text{err}=1$). We compare their uncertainty distributions
using histograms (zoomed to $[0, 0.10]$) and summary statistics.

Key findings: error cells have approximately $10\times$ higher uncertainty standard
deviation and roughly $25\times$ lower rate of near-perfect confidence ($c > 0.99$)
compared to correct cells.
This signal is consistent across both one-hot and scalar encoding families,
suggesting it reflects a genuine property of the model's learned representation
rather than an artifact of the decoding scheme.
